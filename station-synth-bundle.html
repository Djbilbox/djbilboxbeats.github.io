Wavetables taken from real instruments

A wavetable engine is only as good as its tables. Most synths settle for sums of harmonics: clean, and everything ends up sounding related. The 44 tables in STATION SYNTH are built from real spectra — human voice, alto sax, cello, violin, oboe, flute, clarinet, piano, electric piano, organ, clavinet, guitars, double bass, theremin, 8-bit console chips.

Each table sweeps sixteen shapes sampled across the whole family, not the first sixteen that all look alike. The WT POS knob becomes a real morph — from one timbre into another, not a crossfade between two neighbours.

Eleven destinations

Every library is named after a country and every preset after one of its cities. It is not just naming: each library brings three wavetables that are exclusive to it, chosen to match the style — clavinet in France, console chip in Japan, human voice in Algeria.

The engine

Two oscillators

Wavetable morphing, warp, octave, semitones, fine tune, unison up to nine voices with stereo spread.

Sub & noise

A dedicated sub for the low end, a noise generator for attack and texture.

Multimode filter

Cutoff, resonance, drive, key tracking. The drive saturates inside the filter, not after it.

Already have the demo? The upgrade breaks nothing

If the demo is already installed, the bundle also gives you an upgrade installer: it adds the content only — the full factory bank and the eleven libraries — and never touches the plugin. No reinstall, no new plugin scan, no project losing its instrument. You can even run it with your DAW open.

Installation

Windows — a normal .exe installer. The VST3 goes to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, the location every host scans by default, and the standalone app comes with its shortcut.

macOS — a .dmg with the VST3, the Audio Unit (Logic, GarageBand) and the standalone app, as a universal binary for Intel and Apple Silicon. One script puts each format where it belongs.
